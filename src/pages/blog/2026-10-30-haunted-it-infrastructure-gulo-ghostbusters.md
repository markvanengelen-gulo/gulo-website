We've been inside a lot of haunted IT environments. Here's what we keep running into.

## The Haunted House of Horrors: A Tour of Canadian IT Infrastructure

Picture it: you've just taken over IT at a mid-sized Canadian business. You open the network diagram. Except there is no network diagram. There's a sticky note on the router that says *"don't touch — Dave"* and Dave left in 2019.

Welcome to the haunted house.

Every organization has one. It might be a server nobody's rebooted since the Harper government. A cloud bucket named `backup-old-final-FINAL-v3` that contains customer data and has been publicly accessible since 2021. A firewall rule that says `allow all` that someone added to "test something real quick" and then got promoted before removing it.

These aren't edge cases. They are the rule. And like any haunted house, the scariest part isn't what you can see — it's what you can't.

## The Classic IT Ghosts (You Probably Have at Least Two)

**The Zombie Server 🧟**
Technically dead — no one has an active workload on it — but it keeps running. It's consuming power, accumulating vulnerabilities, and occasionally sending mysterious traffic that nobody can explain. Decommissioning it would require understanding what it does, and the person who knew that is LinkedIn-findable but emotionally unreachable.

**The Phantom Admin Account 👻**
Username: `admin`. Password: written on a Post-it inside the server room. Created in 2017 for a contractor who did one project. Still has full domain admin rights. Has never been audited. May or may not have been used by someone who definitely wasn't the contractor last March.

**The Poltergeist Printer 🖨️**
It works fine until the moment you need it. It randomly goes offline during board presentations. It once sent 400 blank pages to itself at 3am and nobody has an explanation. Facilities thinks it's IT's problem. IT thinks it's Facilities' problem. The printer has achieved sentience and is winning.

**The Shadow IT Coven 🧙**
Seventeen SaaS tools your employees signed up for with their work email, none of which went through procurement, two of which are storing customer data in data centres located outside Canada, and one of which the vendor stopped supporting in 2024 but is still technically running. They're all connected to your Google Workspace. Via OAuth. With full access.

## FAQ

**Is my organization's IT actually at risk, or is this just Halloween fun?**
Both, honestly. The monsters in this post are real: unmanaged accounts, unknown cloud exposure, and undocumented infrastructure are among the most common findings in cybersecurity assessments. The spooky metaphors are seasonal. The risk is year-round.

**How would I even know if we have a "phantom admin account" situation?**
A proper identity and access management (IAM) audit will surface stale accounts, over-privileged users, and credentials that haven't been rotated. If you've never done one, assume you have at least a few skeletons.

**We're a small Canadian business — does this really apply to us?**
Ghosts don't check your headcount before haunting. Small and mid-sized organizations are frequently targeted precisely because they're assumed to have weaker controls. The threats are the same; the resources to deal with them are just smaller — which is exactly why outsourcing to a managed IT and security provider makes sense.
